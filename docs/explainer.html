Morse Beacon

Turn an iPhone into a signal lamp.

⚠︎ Photosensitivity warning. This app produces rapid flashing light that may trigger seizures in people with photosensitive epilepsy. Do not use it if you — or anyone who can see the screen — is affected. The app requires an explicit acknowledgement of this warning before it will flash anything. (This page itself does not flash.)

What it is

Morse Beacon is an iPhone app that takes a short message you type and flashes it in International Morse code, using the whole screen as the lamp: pure white for every dit and dah, pure black in between, at maximum brightness. Anyone who can see your screen — across a valley, from a shore, down a street — can read the message with nothing but their eyes and knowledge of the code.

It transmits only. It has no camera, no microphone, no decoder, no history, no network connection and no account. Think of it as the Apple Watch flashlight's search-light mode, given grammar.

When you would use it

Night-time, line-of-sight signaling between people: two hikers on opposite ridges, a boat and a shore, a window and a street. Anywhere a steady flashing light carries further and reads cleaner than a shout — or where silence matters.

How a transmission works

  1. Type the message. Up to 160 characters — letters, digits, and common punctuation. The app checks it as you type and won't transmit anything it can't encode.
  2. Tap Transmit. A 5-second countdown gives you time to aim the phone and warn anyone nearby.
  3. Aim. The screen flashes the message. A dim strip at the top shows your text and its Morse, with the current character highlighted in amber exactly in step with the flash — so you always know what the lamp is "saying".
  4. Tap anywhere to stop — instantly, at any moment. When the message completes, one tap repeats it.

Timing you can trust

Morse is readable only if the rhythm is right: the gap inside a letter must be reliably shorter than the gap between letters. The app schedules every flash against absolute clock deadlines (not "sleep and hope"), so the pattern stays crisp even while the phone is busy drawing. Speeds follow the standard PARIS convention (5–20 words per minute), plus the Farnsworth mode learners prefer: crisp full-speed characters with stretched pauses in between.

Private by construction

No network code, no analytics, no permissions prompts, no accounts — verified by automated checks on every change, not just promised. The only thing the app remembers is your last message and your settings, on your device. A beacon you can trust is one that obviously cannot be doing anything else.

Get it

Morse Beacon is distributed as source code — you build it onto your own phone with Xcode; no App Store involved. The step-by-step guide (including the free-Apple-ID route) is in install-on-device.md; the code, and the reasoning behind it, lives at github.com/y-a-v-a/flashed.
